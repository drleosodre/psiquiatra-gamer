007 First Light: what you have to become to earn a number

Leonardo Sodré · psychiatrist, psychoanalyst and gamer ~5 min read

The Game Awards 2026 on the couch · text 4 of 8
A special series on the blog: one piece on the nominations controversy and one on each of the six Game of the Year nominees, up to the ceremony on December 10. Just arrived? Start with text 1 or read the previous one, text 3.

"Earn the Number" is the tagline IO Interactive chose for 007 First Light, released on May 27, 2026 for PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. The game's James Bond is 26, a Royal Navy air crewman, and not yet 007. Recruited by MI6, he enters the newly revived 00 programme under the guidance of John Greenway, a former 00 agent played by Lennie James. Patrick Gibson plays the lead. Critics liked it: in launch week the game stood at 88 on Metacritic, the highest score in the studio's history. It is now up for Game of the Year and three other categories, including best performance, for Gibson.

It is an origin story, and every origin story answers the same question: what someone has to become to deserve a name and a number. In a spy's case, the answer runs through masks.

A character who arrives before the man

The starting point is a curious one. Everyone already knows Bond, and the game has to invent a Bond who is not that Bond yet. TechRadar's review describes the young man as cocky and impulsive, a Bond from before the polished charm and cold professionalism fully set in.

Notice what this means. The place exists before the person who will fill it. The number is waiting, with the suit, the car and the repertoire, and it is the 26-year-old who has to fit into it, not the other way round.

What Winnicott called the false self

In 1960, the English psychoanalyst Donald Winnicott wrote "Ego Distortion in Terms of True and False Self", a paper collected in 1965 in The Maturational Processes and the Facilitating Environment. The concept has come up here before, in the piece on streamers' personas. It is worth going back to the paper itself, because it is more precise and less moralistic than the term suggests.

The false self is not a lie. Winnicott writes that its defensive function is to hide and protect the true self. Its origin lies at the very start of life: when the environment cannot meet the baby's spontaneous gesture and puts its own gesture in its place, a gesture the baby is meant to validate by going along with it, compliance is born, which he calls the earliest stage of the false self. The baby survives but, in the paper's blunt formula, lives falsely. In the extreme cases, imitation becomes a speciality.

The most useful thing for reading Bond, though, is that Winnicott describes degrees. At one extreme, the false self sets up as real, and it is this that observers take to be the person; in living relationships, at work and in friendships, it begins to fail, because something essential is missing when a whole person is expected. At the other extreme, that of health, the false self is the polite social manner, "not wearing the heart on the sleeve", and the gain is a place in society that the true self alone could never attain or maintain.

Espionage, the false self's profession

It is hard to find a better definition of the trade. A spy only works if he keeps his heart off his sleeve. In the stealth side of 007 First Light, according to GamesRadar+'s review, the player gets past security checkpoints using distractions, disguises and overheard information, in a stealth system with degrees of social manipulation. The same review notes that Bond games used to be shooters at heart, with the world handled through the gun; here, stealth goes hand in hand with charm and the manipulation of people. The mask is not decoration on the character, it is a game mechanic.

And Winnicott's "place in society" takes on a literal form: three digits that open doors the name James Bond, on its own, would not. The false self comes in as the price of admission. The question is how much it charges afterwards.

The gesture the programme has to meet

Winnicott writes that the spontaneous gesture is the true self in action, and that only the true self can be creative and only the true self can feel real. The line sheds light on the game's design. GamesRadar+ highlights the improvised melee combat, the brawl that makes use of whatever is at hand. At the same time, the game takes a strict reading of the licence to kill: Bond only draws his weapon after the enemy shows hostile intent.

This is Winnicott's dilemma of the environment, transposed to an institution. The good-enough environment meets the gesture and, to some extent, makes sense of it. The one that is not good enough swaps the gesture for its own and expects compliance. A secret service has every reason to want compliance, but an agent who only obeys is predictable, and in this trade predictability is fatal. The number, paradoxically, has to go to someone who has not lost the gesture.

Those who can be themselves and also act

There is a passage in the paper that works almost as an entrance exam. Winnicott notes that the false self can form the basis of a kind of sublimation, as when a child grows up to be an actor. Among actors, he says, there are those who can be themselves and also act, and there are others who can only act and who are completely at a loss when not in a role, when not being appreciated or applauded, that is, acknowledged as existing.

The spy who deserves a number is of the first kind. The one of the second kind becomes the character and pays with what Winnicott calls a feeling of unreality or a sense of futility. There is one more detail: in health, he writes, the compromise stops being allowable when the issues become crucial, and then the true self is able to override the compliant self. An origin story is the ideal place to watch for that point, before the mask sticks to the face.

The question holds outside any secret service. What character did you have to become to be accepted somewhere, a job, a family, a group of friends, and how much of it stayed once the badge was already pinned to your chest? And when no one is watching or applauding, do you still know how to step out of the role?
