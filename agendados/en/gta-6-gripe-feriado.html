The "GTA flu": the holiday nobody declared

Leonardo Sodré · psychiatrist, psychoanalyst and gamer ~5 min read

Psychoanalytic countdown to GTA 6 · text 3 · 1 day to go
This is a special series on the blog, one piece a day up to the launch of GTA 6. Just arrived? Start with text 10 or read the previous one, text 4.

On November 10, Logitech announced that its 7,000 employees would get a day off to play video games. It is officially called Logi Game Day. Staff can take it any time until next November, but the company scheduled a livestream at its Americas headquarters for November 19, and the date, Fortune notes, is no coincidence: it is the day GTA 6 comes out on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

That official day off arrives to compete with another one nobody authorized: the "GTA flu," the sudden illness expected to keep a slice of the workforce in bed on launch morning.

An epidemic with a set date

In October, a survey by Zety, reported by HR Dive, found that 11% of workers were planning to take time off or adjust their schedules for GTA 6. Economist José García Montalvo, of Pompeu Fabra University in Barcelona, projected around 1.5 million absences and roughly $1 billion in lost US output in a single day; IBTimes, which ran the numbers, stresses that this is an academic projection, not an official forecast.

Sony and Microsoft have said GTA 6 is the game with the most advance orders in the history of their digital stores. And according to data cited by Fortune, half of the gamers who plan to skip work for a release intend simply to call in sick.

Freud and the festivals ordained by law

Freud has a passage that reads as if it were written for this particular Thursday. It sits in chapter XI of Group Psychology and the Analysis of the Ego, from 1921, and it turns on the ego ideal, the inner yardstick we measure ourselves against. Freud describes it there as the sum of all the limitations the ego has to acquiesce in. In the same chapter he says the sense of guilt can be understood as an expression of tension between the two.

Before he gets there, Freud recalls that some achievements cannot be endured all the time: at birth we trade a state free of stimulation for the outside world, yet we go back to it every night, in sleep; and wit and humor are artifices that let the repressed into the ego for a while, to the increase of our pleasure.

Then comes the application. It is quite conceivable, Freud writes, that the separation of the ego ideal from the ego cannot be borne for long either and has to be temporarily undone. In all the renunciations and limitations imposed on the ego, a periodic infringement of the prohibition is the rule. Proof of this is the institution of festivals, which in origin are nothing other than "excesses provided by law," as Strachey's 1922 translation puts it (vom Gesetz gebotene Exzesse in the German), and which owe their cheerful character to that release. The Roman Saturnalia and our modern carnival, he adds, share this essential feature. And since the ideal gathers up every limitation, setting it aside would be "a magnificent festival for the ego," which might then once again feel satisfied with itself.

A private carnival

The GTA flu is a holiday nobody declared. There is no law and no official calendar, and still a great many people, each in their own corner, picked the same date to suspend the rule. My reading: it is a private carnival on a shared schedule.

Notice the detail that gives it its flavor. Whoever calls in sick does not challenge the norm; they confirm it. Nobody phones in to say they are staying home because they want to play. The excuse speaks the language of the ideal, in which only illness justifies missing work. If festivals owe their cheer to release, something has to be holding you in. Perhaps part of the pleasure of Thursday morning lies less in the game than in the old thrill of playing hooky.

This is different from what I wrote in The guilt of gaming as an adult, about the voice that nags anyone who plays on an ordinary Tuesday, an individual, everyday guilt. Here the phenomenon is periodic and shared: the point is not to silence the voice but to give it a day off, arranged without arranging anything with millions of strangers.

When the company declares the holiday

Then along comes Logitech, and the excess becomes policy, almost to the letter. Its CEO said it is time to retire the stereotype that gaming is a waste of time; its chief people officer, that play is not the opposite of work and that people come back from it with clearer focus. In Germany, a three-day online course on open-world games, running November 18 to 20, makes use of Bildungsurlaub, the paid leave for continuing education, and promises lessons in storytelling, digital culture and collaboration; launch day falls on day two.

Nothing against the day off. But notice what changes. The Saturnalia suspended the order; Logi Game Day and the German course justify play by appealing to the order. The free day comes with a promise of performance, of skills, of focus once you are back. The ego ideal does not step offstage so the ego can celebrate: it approves the party, as long as the party pays off.

And when the ideal signs off on it, what is left of the transgression? A granted day off is legitimate rest. It just is not carnival. By Freud's reasoning, the joy of the festival comes from putting down, for a while, the yardstick the ego measures itself by. A day off that has to prove it is useful is still holding the yardstick.

Thursday, Friday and Monday

In Brazil, launch day is a Thursday, and Friday, November 20, has been a national holiday since Law 14,759 of 2023: the National Day of Zumbi and Black Consciousness, a day of remembrance with a meaning of its own that has nothing to do with the game.

On Thursday, a lot of people's private carnival will have its day. In Freud's logic, the infringement is periodic: the rule comes back afterward, and perhaps it is precisely because it comes back that the festival is any fun. So here is a question that reaches beyond Leonida: where does your carnival live, in what you are allowed to do or in what you grant yourself without asking? And if your favorite transgression came with written permission, would it still taste the same?
